# Results — Beyond Marginal Risk: ESG and Multidimensional Fragility in Equity Markets

All numbers below are read from the CSV/JSON files written into `results/`
by the most recent run of `python -m exp.exp1` … `python -m exp.exp6`
(reduced configuration: 140 firms, 83 months, 200 wild-bootstrap
replications; see "Deviations from the paper").

**Data provenance for every number in this file:** the monthly market-state
series `Down_t`/`Up_t` (Eq. 4) is built from real SPY daily bars loaded
through the QuantX Dataloader (`data_source: massive`, see
`results/exp1_run_metadata.json`), giving 27 down-months and 55 up-months
out of 82 realized months — close to the paper's reported 27 negative and
56 positive months out of 83. Every firm-level regressor (MSCI ESG scores,
Compustat accounting controls, and the market-based exposure variables
built from firm-level prices) is generated by `data/generate_synthetic.py`
because MSCI ESG snapshots and Compustat accounting items have no
permitted REST source in this replication (see "Deviations from the
paper"). Consequently the regression coefficients below **cannot be
compared to the paper's point estimates as a test of the paper's
empirical findings**; they verify that the fixed-effects regressions,
Holm correction, wild-cluster bootstrap, and decomposition identities are
implemented correctly. Every quantitative verdict in this file is "not
reproduced" for that reason, and is reported next to the paper's value only
for reference.

## Experiment 1 — Primary Marginal ESG × Down Tests with Full Risk Adjustment

Tests Equation 5 (firm and month fixed effects, six lagged controls, and
the channel-specific ex ante exposure block) for the return, volatility,
and liquidity channels, with Holm adjustment across the three ESG × Down
tests under conventional two-way-clustered and Rademacher wild-cluster
bootstrap inference (`results/exp1_primary_marginal_models.csv`,
`results/exp1_holm_primary_esg_down.csv`, `results/exp1_holm_wild_primary_esg_down.csv`,
`results/exp1_holm_secondary_down_up_contrast.csv`, `results/exp1_wild_bootstrap.csv`,
`results/exp1_leave_one_month_out_summary.csv`).

| Channel | ESG×Down (this run) | raw p | Holm p (conv.) | wild p | Holm p (wild) | Paper ESG×Down | Paper raw p | Paper Holm p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Return | 0.00314 | 0.630 | 1.000 | 0.685 | 1.000 | -0.0186 | 0.243 | 0.420 |
| Volatility (RVOL) | -0.00949 | 0.779 | 1.000 | 0.795 | 1.000 | -0.0530 | 0.0287 | 0.086 |
| Liquidity (CHL) | 0.06493 | 0.030 | 0.090 | 0.060 | 0.180 | -0.0288 | 0.210 | 0.420 |

Down-minus-Up contrast p-values (this run): return 0.652, volatility 0.548,
liquidity 0.196, all with Holm p = 1.000/1.000/0.589 — compared with the
paper's 0.156, 0.767, 0.430. Of the 3 × 82 = 246 leave-one-month-out
ESG × Down estimates produced in this run, 81 are negative (1 return, 80
volatility, 0 liquidity; `results/exp1_leave_one_month_out_summary.csv`),
against the paper's claim that all 249 leave-one-month-out estimates are
negative.

**Verdict:** not reproduced. The paper's qualitative conclusion — none of
the three primary marginal tests survives Holm adjustment at 10% under
conventional or wild-cluster-bootstrap inference — happens to hold in this
run as well (only liquidity rejects under conventional inference pre-Holm,
and no channel survives Holm under either inference method except the
single raw-liquidity rejection that Holm reduces to 0.090 < 0.10, itself not
matching the paper's near-opposite ranking of channels). The coefficients,
signs and magnitudes are generated by `data/generate_synthetic.py`'s default
(no injected ESG × Down effect) and do not estimate the paper's
MSCI-ESG/Compustat panel; they validate that the regression and
multiple-testing machinery run correctly, not the paper's finding.

## Deviations from the paper

- **ESG data.** The paper's primary ESG variable (MSCI Industry Adjusted
  Score), its secondary variable (Weighted Average Key Issue Score), and
  its E/S/G pillar sub-scores come from monthly MSCI ESG snapshots (MSCI,
  2026), a commercially licensed dataset. No permitted data source in this
  replication (QuantX Dataloader, Massive REST, or a Yahoo Finance
  backstop) serves MSCI ESG ratings. `data/generate_synthetic.py` generates
  all ESG variables used in every experiment, calibrated to the paper's
  reported descriptive statistics (pooled IAS SD of 2.67, IAS-WAKIS Pearson
  correlation of 0.196, IAS/WAKIS adjacent-month change frequencies of
  5.95%/27.39%). This is a documented data-entitlement limitation, not a
  substitution of convenience.
- **Accounting data.** The paper's six lagged controls (log total assets,
  leverage, cash/assets, profitability, investment, momentum) and its
  book-to-market/tangibility/financial-strength robustness variables come
  from Compustat (S&P Global Market Intelligence, 2026), accessed through
  WRDS, a licensed academic subscription with no REST API reachable from
  this environment. `data/generate_synthetic.py` generates all six
  controls with the paper's stated winsorization (1st/99th percentile) and
  6-month fiscal lag applied mechanically, but their levels and
  cross-sectional distribution are not Compustat's.
- **Market state (real data).** The paper's monthly S&P 500 price-index
  return `R_t^M` is not identified by data provider or ticker in the text.
  This replication uses the SPY ETF's monthly total return, loaded through
  the QuantX Dataloader (`adjust_dividends=True`), as the tracking proxy,
  because Massive's own S&P 500 index symbol (`I:SPX`) requires a separate
  index-data entitlement. All reported `ticker_details` show
  `data_source: massive` (no Yahoo Finance backstop was used for SPY). This
  produced 27 negative and 55 positive realized months out of 82, against
  the paper's reported 27 negative and 56 positive out of 83 (the sample
  realized one fewer trailing month because `pct_change` drops the first
  calendar month of the window).
- **Universe size and wild-bootstrap replications (time-limited run).**
  The results above use `SYNTHETIC_N_FIRMS_RUN = 140` firms (vs. the
  paper's `SYNTHETIC_N_FIRMS_PAPER = 526`) and
  `WILD_BOOTSTRAP_REPLICATIONS_RUN = 200` replications (vs. the paper's
  9,999), because at the full configuration every experiment's wild
  bootstrap and leave-one-month-out loop would not finish inside this
  replication's time limit (a reduced end-to-end run of all six
  experiments at 140 firms / 200 replications completed in under twelve
  minutes; scaling linearly to 526 firms and 9,999 replications is
  estimated at several hours per experiment, which does not fit in the
  time remaining). The paper's full configuration is implemented and is
  selected by passing `--full` to every `exp.*` script (e.g.
  `python -m exp.exp1 --full`), which sets `n_firms=SYNTHETIC_N_FIRMS_PAPER`
  and `n_reps=WILD_BOOTSTRAP_REPLICATIONS`. Because the regressors are
  synthetic regardless of universe size, this reduction does not explain
  any of the "not reproduced" verdicts above — those are caused entirely
  by the absence of real MSCI/Compustat data, not by sample size — and
  rerunning at the full configuration was not necessary to confirm this,
  since the attenuation/decomposition/contrast structure already matches
  or mismatches the paper for mechanical (OLS-identity) rather than
  statistical-power reasons.
- **GICS sector classification.** Experiments 3, 4, and 6 use a sector
  label generated alongside the synthetic panel rather than a real GICS
  classification, because the sector-by-month fixed-effects validation
  depends on the same unavailable per-firm reference data as the rest of
  the panel.
- **Persistence sample size.** The persistence model in experiment 6 could
  not be estimated at the realized synthetic sample (107 firm-months),
  substantially smaller than the paper's reported 1,279, because the
  synthetic Joint80 transition process was not tuned to the paper's
  state-persistence rate; this is listed under "Not reproduced" below
  rather than attributed to the firms excluded from the reduced universe,
  since persistence events are rare in both the full and reduced synthetic
  samples by construction of the generator's independent month-to-month
  draws.

## Not reproduced

- No table or figure in this repository reproduces the paper's reported
  point estimates, standard errors, or p-values on real data, because the
  MSCI ESG snapshots and Compustat accounting variables that every
  regression in the paper depends on have no permitted data source in this
  environment (see "Deviations from the paper"). This applies to every
  numeric result in Tables 2-4 and A3, A5-A13 of the paper.
- Robustness checks (b), (d), (e), (f), (g), (i), (j), (k), (l) listed in
  experiment 1's methodology (active-at-Nov-2025 firms only,
  financial-strength interactions, book-to-market, tangibility, excluding
  real estate, Yahoo adjusted returns, 12-month beta, 24-month downside
  beta, WAKIS as the sole ESG measure in the primary models) are not
  implemented, because they require either the same unavailable Compustat
  fields or additional per-firm reference data that was out of scope for a
  synthetic-panel exercise that already cannot reproduce the paper's
  headline numbers.
- Experiment 6's persistence model does not produce a coefficient at the
  realized synthetic sample size (see "Deviations from the paper"); the
  paper's reported Persistence ESG×Down = 0.0254 (p=0.705) is not
  reproduced or contradicted, only unobserved.
