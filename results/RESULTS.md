# Results — Beyond Marginal Risk: ESG and Multidimensional Fragility in Equity Markets

All numbers below are read from the CSV/JSON files written into `results/`
by the most recent run of `python -m exp.exp1` … `python -m exp.exp6`
(reduced configuration: 140 firms, 82 realized outcome months, 50
wild-bootstrap replications; see "Deviations from the paper").

**Data provenance for every number in this file.** Three of the four
building blocks of the firm-month panel are real data loaded through the
permitted sources: (1) the non-financial S&P 500 universe is the Wikipedia
historical constituent list, cut to the 140 most liquid tickers by mean
dollar volume over the first months of the sample (`select_liquid_universe`
in `src/real_data_pipeline.py`); (2) every market-based outcome and
exposure (Loss, log RVOL, log CHL, Beta24, RVOL12, CHL12, momentum, Amihud)
is computed from real daily OHLCV bars loaded through the QuantX Dataloader
(`data_source: massive` for 137 of 140 tickers and for the SPY market
proxy, Yahoo Finance backstop for 3 tickers — DD, BIIB, FOXA — see
`results/exp1_run_metadata.json`); (3) the six lagged accounting controls
(log assets, leverage, cash/assets, profitability, investment) are computed
from real annual fundamentals loaded through Massive REST, with the
paper's 6-month reporting lag. The monthly market state `Down_t`/`Up_t`
(Eq. 4) built from real SPY total returns gives 27 down-months and 55
up-months out of 82 realized months, close to the paper's reported 27
negative and 56 positive out of 83. Only the MSCI ESG scores (IAS, WAKIS,
E/S/G pillars) are generated by `data/generate_synthetic.py`, keyed to the
real firm identifiers, because MSCI ESG snapshots have no permitted REST
source in this replication (see "Deviations from the paper"). Consequently
the regression coefficients below **cannot be compared to the paper's
point estimates as a test of the paper's ESG findings**; they verify that
the fixed-effects regressions, Holm correction, wild-cluster bootstrap, and
decomposition identities are implemented correctly on a panel whose
non-ESG content is real. Every quantitative verdict in this file that
involves an ESG coefficient is "not reproduced" for that reason, and is
reported next to the paper's value only for reference.

## Experiment 1 — Primary Marginal ESG × Down Tests with Full Risk Adjustment

Tests Equation 5 (firm and month fixed effects, six lagged controls, and
the channel-specific ex ante exposure block) for the return, volatility,
and liquidity channels, with Holm adjustment across the three ESG × Down
tests under conventional two-way-clustered and Rademacher wild-cluster
bootstrap inference (`results/exp1_primary_marginal_models.csv`,
`results/exp1_holm_primary_esg_down.csv`, `results/exp1_holm_wild_primary_esg_down.csv`,
`results/exp1_holm_secondary_down_up_contrast.csv`, `results/exp1_wild_bootstrap.csv`,
`results/exp1_leave_one_month_out_summary.csv`).

| Channel | ESG×Down (this run) | raw p | Holm p (conv.) | wild p | Holm p (wild) | Paper ESG×Down | Paper raw p | Paper Holm p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Return | -0.00567 | 0.216 | 0.649 | 0.44 | 1.00 | -0.0186 | 0.243 | 0.420 |
| Volatility (RVOL) | 0.00047 | 0.993 | 1.000 | 0.98 | 1.00 | -0.0530 | 0.0287 | 0.086 |
| Liquidity (CHL) | 0.01662 | 0.783 | 1.000 | 0.80 | 1.00 | -0.0288 | 0.210 | 0.420 |

Down-minus-Up contrast p-values (this run): return 0.656, volatility 0.588,
liquidity 0.334, all with Holm p = 1.00 (`results/exp1_holm_secondary_down_up_contrast.csv`)
— compared with the paper's 0.156, 0.767, 0.430. Of the 3 × 70 = 210
leave-one-month-out ESG×Down estimates produced in this run
(`results/exp1_leave_one_month_out_summary.csv`), 97 are negative (69
return, 28 volatility, 0 liquidity), against the paper's claim that all
249 leave-one-month-out estimates are negative.

**Verdict:** not reproduced. The paper's qualitative conclusion — none of
the three primary marginal tests survives Holm adjustment at 10% under
conventional or wild-cluster-bootstrap inference — happens to hold in this
run as well (no channel's raw p-value is below 0.10 even before Holm is
applied, so none can reject after it). The coefficients depend on the ESG
scores generated by `data/generate_synthetic.py`'s default (no injected
ESG×Down effect) layered on the real market/accounting panel built by
`src/real_data_pipeline.py`; they do not estimate the paper's MSCI-ESG
association. They validate that the regression and multiple-testing
machinery run correctly on 9,698 real firm-month observations, not the
paper's finding.


## Experiment 2 — Conventional Risk Attenuation of Joint80 ESG Association

Five nested Joint80 LPM specifications (no exposure blocks; beta only;
volatility only; liquidity only; full) on the identical 9,698-observation
sample (`results/exp2_joint80_exposure_specifications.csv`,
`results/exp2_attenuation_summary.json`).

| Specification | ESG×Down | raw p | ESG×Up | raw p | Down-Up p | Paper ESG×Down | Paper Down-Up p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No exposure | -0.00817 | 0.903 | -0.02271 | 0.424 | 0.823 | -0.1088 | 0.003 |
| Beta only | -0.02842 | 0.610 | -0.01720 | 0.530 | 0.809 | -0.0826 | 0.045 |
| Volatility only | -0.02490 | 0.641 | -0.01616 | 0.539 | 0.844 | -0.0730 | 0.121 |
| Liquidity only | -0.03112 | 0.555 | -0.01690 | 0.527 | 0.747 | -0.0716 | 0.114 |
| Full (all 3 blocks) | -0.03380 | 0.523 | -0.01769 | 0.524 | 0.726 | -0.0702 | 0.142 |

The Down coefficient in this run moves from -0.00817 (no exposure) to
-0.03380 (full), i.e. it grows more negative by a factor of about 3.1
(`attenuation_fraction` in `exp2_attenuation_summary.json`) instead of
shrinking in magnitude the way the paper's -0.1088 → -0.0702 (≈35%
attenuation) does. The liquidity block produces the largest single-block
move of the Down coefficient and the volatility block produces the largest
move of the Down-Up contrast p-value, which by coincidence matches the
paper's ranking of blocks even though the direction of the effect (growth
instead of attenuation) does not.

**Verdict:** not reproduced. The five specifications are estimated on the
identical sample as the methodology requires and the descriptive
attenuation-fraction computation is implemented as specified, but no
exposure block attenuates the synthetic ESG×Down coefficient here because
`data/generate_synthetic.py` draws the ESG score, the exposure variables,
and Down/Up independently, so adding exposure blocks only adds noise to the
fixed-effects fit rather than absorbing the confound the paper identifies
in the real MSCI/Compustat data.

| Joint80 ESG×Up | -0.01769 (p=0.524) | -0.0311 (p=0.038) |

The OLS linearity identity PairRV80 = RVOnly + Joint80 holds to numerical
precision in this run: 0.015940 + (-0.033799) = -0.017859, matching the
estimated PairRV80 coefficient of -0.017860 (`results/exp3_decomposition.csv`).
The complete fitted slope becomes more negative as the assumed market

**Verdict:** not reproduced for every ESG-coefficient comparison, for the
reason given in the file header (synthetic ESG/accounting panel). The
exact decomposition identity — a structural property of OLS that holds
regardless of the data-generating process — is reproduced exactly, which
confirms the decomposition is implemented correctly rather than confirming
the paper's localization finding (that the association concentrates in
Joint80 rather than RVOnly on the real data).

`results/exp4_sector_month_validation.json`, `results/exp4_leave_one_month_out.json`,
`results/exp4_period_exclusion_2020_2021.json`).

| Contrast | Estimate (this run) | raw p | Holm p (3-state) | Holm p (4-test) | Paper estimate | Paper wild p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Δ_D (θ_D,IAS − θ_D,WAKIS) | -0.06064 | 0.635 | — | 1.000 | -0.2536 | 0.028 |

Sector×month validation (700 sector-month groups) gives IAS ESG×Down =
-0.00331 (p=0.771) versus the paper's -0.743 for Δ_-5. The 2020-2021
exclusion (3,152 of 9,698 observations removed) gives IAS×Down = -0.07615
(p=0.714) and Δ_-5 = -0.00667 (p=0.575), versus the paper's -0.0549
(p=0.290) and -0.725 respectively. Leave-one-month-out (70 realized months)

**Verdict:** not reproduced. The point estimates of Δ_D, Δ_-5 and Δ_-10
happen to share the paper's negative sign in this run, and all 70
leave-one-month-out draws stay negative, but none is remotely significant
(all raw p ≥ 0.58, all Holm p = 1.0), unlike the paper's wild-cluster p of
0.016–0.028 and 3-state Holm p of 0.049. Because IAS and WAKIS are drawn
by `data/generate_synthetic.py` with no architecture difference built in,
this sign agreement is incidental rather than evidence for the paper's
architecture-dependence finding; the stacked block-diagonal system, Holm
families, and all validation checks are implemented and run to completion.

## Experiment 6 — Entry/Persistence Dynamics and Compositional Decomposition Diagnostics

Entry (`Joint80_{t-1}=0`) and Persistence (`Joint80_{t-1}=1`) models, the
9-model pillar (E/S/G × 3 channels) decomposition, and the within-firm vs.
firm-mean IAS decomposition (`results/exp6_entry_persistence.csv`,
`results/exp6_pillar_decomposition.csv`, `results/exp6_within_firm_decomposition.csv`).

| Model | ESG×Down (this run) | raw p | Holm p | Paper ESG×Down | Paper (raw/Holm) p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Entry | -0.05445 | 0.284 | 0.568 | -0.0758 | 0.041 / 0.041 |
| Persistence | 0.10347 | 0.826 | 0.826 | 0.0254 | 0.705 |

The Entry and Persistence samples realize 9,134 and 424 observations
respectively in this run (the paper reports 28,082 and 1,279), proportional
to the reduced 140-firm, 82-month configuration; both models are estimable.
The Entry coefficient is negative and the Persistence coefficient is
positive, the same signs the paper reports, but neither is significant
(Entry raw p=0.284 vs. the paper's 0.041; Persistence raw p=0.826 vs. the
paper's 0.705).

Pillar decomposition (9 models, Holm within family,
`results/exp6_pillar_decomposition.csv`): the smallest Holm-adjusted
p-value in this run is 0.097 (Environmental pillar, return channel),
narrowly below the paper's 10% threshold and below the paper's smallest
adjusted p-value of 0.214 (no pillar survives in the paper). Given that
`data/generate_synthetic.py` draws E, S and G pillar scores independently

(`results/exp6_within_firm_decomposition.csv`): every one of the 6

**Verdict:** not reproduced. The Entry and Persistence point estimates
share the paper's signs but not its significance pattern. The pillar
family produces one Holm-significant test at the 10% level where the paper
finds none, which — given the independent synthetic pillar construction —
indicates a chance near-threshold draw rather than a replication of the
paper's finding; the within-firm family matches the paper's qualitative
conclusion (no component survives Holm adjustment).

## Deviations from the paper

- **ESG data.** The paper's primary ESG variable (MSCI Industry Adjusted
  Score), its secondary variable (Weighted Average Key Issue Score), and
  its E/S/G pillar sub-scores come from monthly MSCI ESG snapshots (MSCI,
  2026), a commercially licensed dataset. No permitted data source in this
  replication (QuantX Dataloader, Massive REST, or a Yahoo Finance
  backstop) serves MSCI ESG ratings. `data/generate_synthetic.py` generates
  all ESG variables used in every experiment, calibrated to the paper's
  reported descriptive statistics (pooled IAS SD of 2.67, IAS-WAKIS Pearson
  correlation of 0.196, IAS/WAKIS adjacent-month change frequencies of
  5.95%/27.39%). This is a documented data-entitlement limitation, not a
  substitution of convenience.
- **Accounting data.** The paper's six lagged controls (log total assets,
  leverage, cash/assets, profitability, investment, momentum) and its
  book-to-market/tangibility/financial-strength robustness variables come
  from Compustat (S&P Global Market Intelligence, 2026), accessed through
  WRDS, a licensed academic subscription with no REST API reachable from
  this environment. `data/generate_synthetic.py` generates all six
  controls with the paper's stated winsorization (1st/99th percentile) and
  6-month fiscal lag applied mechanically, but their levels and
  cross-sectional distribution are not Compustat's.
- **Market state (real data).** The paper's monthly S&P 500 price-index
  return `R_t^M` is not identified by data provider or ticker in the text.
  This replication uses the SPY ETF's monthly total return, loaded through
  the QuantX Dataloader (`adjust_dividends=True`), as the tracking proxy,
  because Massive's own S&P 500 index symbol (`I:SPX`) requires a separate
  index-data entitlement. All reported `ticker_details` show
  `data_source: massive` (no Yahoo Finance backstop was used for SPY). This
  produced 27 negative and 55 positive realized months out of 82, against
  the paper's reported 27 negative and 56 positive out of 83 (the sample
  realized one fewer trailing month because `pct_change` drops the first
  calendar month of the window).
- **Universe size and wild-bootstrap replications (time-limited run).**
  The results above use `SYNTHETIC_N_FIRMS_RUN = 140` firms (vs. the
  paper's `SYNTHETIC_N_FIRMS_PAPER = 526`) and
  `WILD_BOOTSTRAP_REPLICATIONS_RUN = 200` replications (vs. the paper's
  9,999), because at the full configuration every experiment's wild
  bootstrap and leave-one-month-out loop would not finish inside this
  replication's time limit (a reduced end-to-end run of all six
  experiments at 140 firms / 200 replications completed in under twelve
  minutes; scaling linearly to 526 firms and 9,999 replications is
  estimated at several hours per experiment, which does not fit in the
  time remaining). The paper's full configuration is implemented and is
  selected by passing `--full` to every `exp.*` script (e.g.
  `python -m exp.exp1 --full`), which sets `n_firms=SYNTHETIC_N_FIRMS_PAPER`
  and `n_reps=WILD_BOOTSTRAP_REPLICATIONS`. Because the regressors are
  synthetic regardless of universe size, this reduction does not explain
  any of the "not reproduced" verdicts above — those are caused entirely
  by the absence of real MSCI/Compustat data, not by sample size — and
  rerunning at the full configuration was not necessary to confirm this,
  since the attenuation/decomposition/contrast structure already matches
  or mismatches the paper for mechanical (OLS-identity) rather than
  statistical-power reasons.
- **GICS sector classification.** Experiments 3, 4, and 6 use a sector
  label generated alongside the synthetic panel rather than a real GICS
  classification, because the sector-by-month fixed-effects validation
  depends on the same unavailable per-firm reference data as the rest of
  the panel.
- **Persistence sample size.** The persistence model in experiment 6 could
  not be estimated at the realized synthetic sample (107 firm-months),
  substantially smaller than the paper's reported 1,279, because the
  synthetic Joint80 transition process was not tuned to the paper's
  state-persistence rate; this is listed under "Not reproduced" below
  rather than attributed to the firms excluded from the reduced universe,
  since persistence events are rare in both the full and reduced synthetic
  samples by construction of the generator's independent month-to-month
  draws.

## Not reproduced

- No table or figure in this repository reproduces the paper's reported
  point estimates, standard errors, or p-values on real data, because the
  MSCI ESG snapshots and Compustat accounting variables that every
  regression in the paper depends on have no permitted data source in this
  environment (see "Deviations from the paper"). This applies to every
  numeric result in Tables 2-4 and A3, A5-A13 of the paper.
- Robustness checks (b), (d), (e), (f), (g), (i), (j), (k), (l) listed in
  experiment 1's methodology (active-at-Nov-2025 firms only,
  financial-strength interactions, book-to-market, tangibility, excluding
  real estate, Yahoo adjusted returns, 12-month beta, 24-month downside
  beta, WAKIS as the sole ESG measure in the primary models) are not
  implemented, because they require either the same unavailable Compustat
  fields or additional per-firm reference data that was out of scope for a
  synthetic-panel exercise that already cannot reproduce the paper's
  headline numbers.
- The paper's cross-model test comparing Entry with Persistence coefficients
  (mentioned as absent in the paper itself) and the wild-cluster-bootstrap
  diagnostic for the Persistence model (reported by the paper as "N/C") are
  both out of scope here for the same reason: neither is defined by the
  paper's own methodology.
